Privacy & cookie statement

Last updated 19 November 2026

Who is responsible

This website is my personal, interactive CV. I, Bryce de Bruyn (Barcelona, Spain), am the data controller for it. For any question about your data, email me at debruynbryce2001@gmail.com.

The short version

  • You can read the whole site without accepting any cookies.
  • I use Google Analytics 4 to count visits and see which sections people read. Nothing is used for advertising or to build a profile of you.
  • Until you choose, and if you press Only essential, Google Analytics runs without cookies: it only receives anonymous visit counts that cannot recognise you or link your visits together.
  • If you press Accept, Google Analytics may also set its cookies, so repeat visits can be counted.
  • You can change your choice at any time with the button below or “Cookie settings” at the bottom of the CV.

Google Analytics

Every visit loads Google Analytics 4 through the Google tag, using Google Consent Mode.

Without consent (before you choose, or “Only essential”): no cookies are set or read. Google receives anonymous signals (“cookieless pings”) that a page was viewed or something was clicked, with the page, time, approximate country and city, and your device and browser type, but no ID that recognises you or connects one visit to another. Google uses them only to estimate visitor totals.

With consent (“Accept”): Google Analytics also sets the cookies listed below. It then collects usage data such as the pages and sections viewed, how long you stay, what you click (for example “Download CV”), the website that sent you here, your approximate location (country and city, derived from your IP address), and your device, browser, operating system and screen size. Its cookies give your browser a random ID so repeat visits can be counted.

Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses. Nothing is used for advertising: this site always tells Google that ad storage, ad user data and ad personalisation are refused.

Legal basis: for analytics cookies, your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR and Article 22.2 of the Spanish LSSI). You can withdraw it at any time; this does not affect anything processed before you withdrew it. For the anonymous counts without cookies, my legitimate interest in knowing how many people visit my CV (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can object to it by emailing me, or block it with a browser content blocker.

Who receives the data: Google Ireland Limited (Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland) processes the data for me as a processor. Data may be transferred to Google LLC in the United States, which is certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework; Google’s standard contractual clauses also apply. More: how Google uses data from sites that use its services and Google’s privacy policy.

How long it is kept: Google Analytics keeps visit-level data for 14 months; after that only totals remain.

Other services this site uses

  • Hosting: the site is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.). Like any web server, GitHub receives your IP address to deliver the pages and may log it for security. See GitHub’s privacy statement.
  • Fonts: the typefaces are loaded from Google Fonts, so your browser sends your IP address to Google to download them. Google Fonts does not set cookies. See the Google Fonts privacy FAQ.
  • Embedded projects: the Creative Portfolio and Past Projects sections show two of my own websites (bdbphotography.site and my EasyA project on GitHub Pages). They only load when you scroll to them and do not use analytics cookies.
  • Links: LinkedIn and YouTube are plain links. Nothing from them loads until you click, and their own privacy policies apply once you are on their sites.

The legal basis for hosting and fonts is my legitimate interest in showing the site to you securely and correctly (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access your data, have it corrected or deleted, restrict or object to its use, receive it in a portable format, and withdraw your consent at any time. Because Google Analytics only uses a random ID, I usually cannot link the data to you personally; email me and I’ll do what I can.

You can also complain to a data protection authority. In Spain that is the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (AEPD), or you can contact the authority in the EU country where you live.

Changes

If I change how this site uses data, I’ll update this page and the date at the top.
